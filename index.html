Lexicon — UNL terms in plain English read once · 60 sec

Lexicon
New to ancillary? Read this once. These are the only UNL-specific terms used in the rest of this reference — everything else is plain MA-agent English.
HI · Hospital Indemnity
(HI Shield)
UNL's hospital cash product. Pays you cash directly when you're admitted. Not health insurance — supplemental indemnity.
HHC · Home Healthcare
(HHC Shield)
UNL's home care product. Pays cash when you can't perform 2 of 6 daily-living activities. Includes prescription reimbursement. Not health insurance.
HHC Plan A / B / C
The three HHC tiers — match to the customer's monthly Rx count. Plan A = light (1-2 meds), Plan B = typical fit (3-5 meds), Plan C = heavy (6+ meds).
TCare
The $3,500 caregiver benefit included in HHC Plan B and C. Pays the family member who provides care, not the customer.
Rx reimbursement
A built-in feature of HHC. Customer emails receipts to claims@unlinsurance.com; UNL mails them a check ($10/generic, $25/brand). Up to $50/month for Plan B.
NEPQ
Neuro-Emotional Persuasion Questioning — the discovery-question method we use. Translation: ask the customer questions instead of pitching at them.
ADL
Activity of Daily Living — bathing, dressing, toileting, transferring, eating, continence. HHC triggers when the customer can't perform 2 of these 6.
SOA
Scope of Appointment — the verbal authorization at the start of every CPC MA call. Your existing SOA already covers HI + HHC.
DQ · Mid-call DQ
Disqualification — when a health knockout fires (cancer, insulin diabetes, etc.) and the customer can't qualify for one of the products. Handle gracefully; pivot if possible.
Q1 / Q2 / Q3 / Q4
The four close-sequence questions in order. Q1: "Do you feel like this could be the answer for you?" Q2: "Why do you feel like it is, though?" Q3: "What parts will help you most?" Q4: "Do you feel like this is something you can have?"

Open

2

The 4 Discovery Questions (NEPQ — problem awareness)

Discover

Four open-ended discovery questions (NEPQ-style) that surface real exposures BEFORE you pitch a product. Drop them inside NEADS — woven into the natural rhythm of MA discovery, not stacked at the end.

HI seed
"If you were admitted to the hospital next month, do you know what your plan's copay would be per day? On most MA plans it's $200-400/day. A 4-day stay = $1,200-1,800 out of pocket. How would that feel if it hit your account next month?"
HHC seed
"Different question — have you ever needed help around the house — bathing, dressing, getting around — even temporarily after an illness or surgery? Did you know Medicare doesn't actually cover that kind of daily-living help?"
Family-burden seed
"If you needed help like that for a few months — say after a fall or stroke — who in your life would be doing that for you?"
Past-experience seed
"Has anyone close to you — a parent, sibling, friend — been hospitalized or needed help at home in the last couple years? Do you know what they ended up paying out of pocket?"
Loop their answers: "Say more about that?" The second articulation is always more emotional than the first.
★ The Implication follow-up (use after any seed that lands with concern): "If something like that happened to you and you didn't have a way to cover it — how do you think it would play out?" Forces them to mentally simulate the consequence. NEPQ-master move; prospects who simulate the bad outcome convert at 3-5x the rate of those who only acknowledge the abstract risk.
3

The Preference Question

Discover

The single highest-leverage discovery move on the entire call. Drill this. Ask it between the HI (Hospital Indemnity) and HHC (Home Healthcare) presentations — their answer becomes the anchor for everything that follows.

Verbatim
"If you had an accident or a surgery and later had to choose between recovering in a nursing facility or recovering at home with someone coming over to help you bathe, dress, and get around — which would you want?"
~95% say "home"
Why the specific activities: "Someone coming over to help you bathe, dress, and get around" makes them picture a real person doing real things — not abstract "care." And those three activities ARE the actual HHC trigger ADLs — clever foreshadowing for the product you'll present next.
★ After they say "home" (95% will) — LOOP: "Say more about that — what is it about home that matters to you?" Then stay silent. Whatever they say is THEIR reason. In the close, anchor back: "You said home matters to you because [their words] — that's exactly what this is designed to protect." One loop turns a binary answer into a value-anchor that makes the close inevitable.

★The Rx-Net-Cost Reframe

Pitch

The single most important pitch on the call. Drill it to muscle memory. Gross premium first, then the math that actually matters.

Pre-frame: "Bear with me — I'll give you the gross premium first, then the math that actually matters."
"You're already filling prescriptions. HHC Shield reimburses $10 for every generic, $25 for brand names. You email receipts to UNL; they mail you a check. If you fill 4/month, you typically receive $40-50/month in checks. [pause]

The HHC premium is $53/month — drafts from your account automatically. The reimbursement comes back separately by check after you submit receipts. So $53 out, about $50 back. Net spending around $3-5/month on HHC. [pause]

Hospital Indemnity is a separate supplemental product alongside — about $40/month, no Rx offset, but pays $1,200+ in cash on a typical 4-day admission.

Two separate supplemental products. Combined premium ~$93/month, Rx reimbursement ~$50/month back. Net spending ~$43/month across both — about $1.50/day for hospital cash + home healthcare + $3,500 caregiver benefit. Does that make sense?"
Critical: always say "premium drafts $53" AND "reimbursement comes back separately." Never collapse the two numbers into a single "net" figure — that's a compliance violation. See the Never Say rail.

Mid-Call DQ (Disqualification) — when an HI knockout fires

DQ

A health knockout surfaces mid-call and the customer no longer qualifies for one of the products. You break it gracefully. HI (Hospital Indemnity) off the table — HHC (Home Healthcare) stays on.

"I want to be straight with you. Based on what you just shared, the hospital indemnity piece isn't going to be a fit. The 24-month look-back catches it. Let me keep going with home healthcare — different underwriting, and we can revisit HI in [Y] months."
DQ fast. HHC stays on. Don't lose the call over HI.
7

Pre-emptive Reframes — handle BEFORE the objection surfaces

Preempt

When you hear the cue early, drop the reframe before they object. Defuses the resistance before it forms.

Medigap holder
"Your Medigap is great for what it does — pays the deductibles and coinsurance Medicare leaves on hospital care. You don't need Hospital Indemnity on top of that. But Medigap can't add new benefit categories, so the home care gap is still there. Let me show you Home Healthcare on its own."
"MA already has supplemental benefits"
"Some MA plans do have a small in-home support benefit — usually capped at a small dollar amount, limited to certain chronic conditions, only about 7% of MA plans have a meaningful version of this. Want me to walk through your Summary of Benefits and confirm what your plan actually offers? Then we can see if this complements it or duplicates it."
"I'm in good shape"
"That's exactly why this is the right time to look at it — about 70% of seniors who reach 65 will need help at home at some point. Underwriting tightens dramatically once a health change happens. The window to lock this in at a clean rate is when you're healthy."
"Medicare covers 100 days of home care"
"That's actually the Skilled Nursing Facility rule — for nursing home stays after a hospital admission. Medicare home health has no time limit, but it only covers SKILLED care — nursing visits, physical therapy. It doesn't cover daily-living help like bathing or dressing, which is what Home Healthcare pays for."

Customer Questions — what they'll ask

FAQ

These aren't objections to overcome — they're questions to answer cleanly.

"What if I don't fill prescriptions?"
"Then the Rx reimbursement doesn't apply — you'd just have the home healthcare and hospital coverage. Most agents don't recommend HHC unless you're filling prescriptions, because the math works best with the reimbursement."
"How long until the reimbursement check comes?"
"Typically 2-3 weeks after you email your receipt to UNL. Some go faster."
"Can my spouse get this too?"
"Yes — separate application, separate underwriting. Up to 2 insureds on the same application; each gets their own individual policy."
"What if I cancel?"
"You can cancel anytime by contacting UNL. Premiums paid are generally not refundable after the first month — but you're not locked in."
"What if I move out of state?"
"Coverage follows you. The state rate may adjust at renewal, but the policy stays active."
"How do I file a claim?"
"Hospital Indemnity: call UNL within 30 days of discharge, give them the admission dates and hospital name, they mail you a check. Home Healthcare: when you can't perform 2 of 6 daily activities (bathing, dressing, etc.) or have certified cognitive impairment, your doctor certifies it and UNL pays monthly. We covered this earlier."
"Does this affect my Medicare or my SSI?"
"No on Medicare — your MA stays exactly the same. No on Social Security retirement income either. (If you're on Medicaid or SSI specifically, the cash benefits could affect eligibility — we'd need to check that separately.)"
"Is this through Medicare?"
"No — this is a separate supplemental indemnity policy through UNL Insurance, not part of your Medicare. It works alongside your Medicare; it doesn't change your Medicare at all."
The full script · End-to-end
The verbatim integrated CPC MA + Ancillary call

This is the verbatim end-to-end call flow — both Scenario 3 (MA closes → harvest ancillary) and Scenario 4 (MA fails → pivot to ancillary). Designed to read along during early cross-sell calls until muscle memory builds.

The standard MA portions of Phase 1 are compressed (reference the full MA script); the cross-sell additions are shown in detail. Phase 6 (Harvest) and Phase 6 alt (Pivot) are the centerpiece moves — drill those first.

Compliance reference · Verbatim

TPMO Disclaimer + Verbal SOA (Scope of Appointment)

TPMO (Third-Party Marketing Organization) Disclaimer — within first 60 seconds
"You do not have to provide any health information, unless it's used to determine your eligibility to enroll. We do not offer every plan available in your area. Any information we provide is limited to those plans we do offer in your area. Please contact Medicare.gov or 1-800-MEDICARE to get information on all of your options."
Verbal SOA — your authority for HI (Hospital Indemnity) + HHC (Home Healthcare)
"Clear Path Coverage is contracted by multiple Medicare plan providers and does not work directly for the Federal government. Plans are insured or covered by a Medicare Advantage organization with a Medicare contract and/or a Medicare-approved Part D sponsor. Enrollment in the plan depends on the plan's contract renewal with Medicare. By talking with me, you are under no obligation to enroll, your current or future Medicare enrollment status will not be impacted, and you will not automatically be enrolled in a plan. I do need your permission, to discuss your Medicare Advantage, Hospital Indemnity, Home Healthcare, and prescription drug plan benefits. Do I have your permission to continue?"
The SOA (Scope of Appointment) already authorizes HI + HHC. Cross-sell isn't a new motion — it's the script doing what it was designed to do. No further compliance step needed for the cross-sell.

★THE HARVEST (Scenario 3 — after a successful MA close)

Phase 6
Pivot AFTER MA enrollment is fully complete but BEFORE the Retention Statement

The NEPQ (discovery-questions) master move — delay the price until they want it. Don't drop premium until both products are presented and the Preference Question has landed. The flow below presents HI (Hospital Indemnity) mechanics → checks understanding → uses the Preference Question to surface "home" → presents HHC (Home Healthcare) + Rx mechanics → THEN reveals the combined number in per-day units (≈$1.50/day). Daily-unit framing lands far softer than monthly. They WANT it before they hear what it costs.

Open with HI — the natural conversation entry:

"Now [Name], remember at the start of the call I mentioned the two supplemental products that go alongside your MA? Let me take 5 minutes and walk you through them. Earlier you mentioned [their hospital copay / family-help concern / Rx situation — pick the seed that landed hardest]. I want to show you how this fits.

The first one is called Hospital Indemnity Shield. It's a supplemental indemnity policy — it's not health insurance, it's separate from your Medicare. What it does is pay you cash directly when you're admitted to the hospital. So on a plan like yours with the $300/day copay, if you ended up admitted for 4 days, Hospital Indemnity would mail you a check for $1,200. The money is yours — you can use it for the hospital bill, for household expenses, for anything.

Does that make sense as how it works?"

[Pause. Wait for "yes" or a clarifying question. Loop if they say anything that hints at concern. Don't mention the premium yet. You're checking comprehension of the mechanic — not selling on price.]

Bridge to HHC with the Preference Question — get them to verbalize "home" before you offer the home solution:

"Now, there's a second supplemental product that pairs alongside it for what happens AFTER the hospital. Before I show you how it works — quick question:

If you had an accident or a surgery and later had to choose between recovering in a nursing facility or recovering at home with someone coming over to help you bathe, dress, and get around — which would you want?"

[They'll say "home" — about 95% do. Loop it: "Say more about that — why home?" Their answer becomes the anchor for the HHC presentation. They just told you they want the thing HHC pays for.]

Layer HHC — anchor to their answer:

"Right — and that's what most folks want. The catch is, Medicare doesn't actually cover daily-living help at home. That's true on Original Medicare, on Medicare Advantage, on Medigap — none of them cover it. So if you came home from the hospital weak — after a fall, or a stroke, or surgery — and you needed help bathing, dressing, getting around for a few weeks, that's all out of pocket. Or your family steps in.

The product is called Home Healthcare Shield. Also a supplemental indemnity policy, not health insurance. It pays you cash for daily-living help — $300 a day for up to 360 days if you qualify. Plus there's a $3,500 caregiver benefit that pays directly to a family member who helps you.

And there's something built into Home Healthcare that makes the math really work — a prescription reimbursement. Every time you fill a prescription, you email the receipt to UNL — they reimburse you $10 for every generic, $25 for brand names. So if you're filling 4 medications a month, you typically receive checks totaling $40-50 a month. Does that make sense?"

[Pause again. Confirm they understand HHC + Rx mechanic. Still no premium.]

NOW reveal the combined math — in per-day units:

"OK so putting both products together — Hospital Indemnity for the admission, Home Healthcare for the recovery, plus the $3,500 caregiver benefit and the prescription reimbursement that funds most of it — here's the math.

The combined premium across both products is about $93 a month, drafted from your account. The Rx reimbursement of about $50 a month comes back to you separately, by check, after you submit receipts. So your net spending across both products lands around $43 a month — about a dollar fifty a day. Less than a cup of coffee.

These are two separate supplemental products. You can enroll in either one, both, or neither — they're independent.

How does that fit?"

Why this lands: The price hits AFTER they've heard the value and verbalized that they'd want to recover at home. By the time they hear "$1.50/day," their internal voice has already said "I'd want this." Daily-unit framing (vs. monthly) cuts perceived cost roughly in half emotionally. This is the highest-conversion sequence in the playbook.

Run the Close Sequence (Q1-Q4):

  • Q1: "Do you feel like this could be the answer for you?" [Stop talking. Wait.]
  • Q2: "Hold on — why do you feel like it is, though?" [Silent. They sell themselves.]
  • Q3: "What specific parts of what we went over today feel like they'd help you the most?"
  • Q4: "Do you feel like this is something you can have that'll get you where you want to go?"
  • "Really, the next step is just confirming the application — takes about 10 minutes. Sound fair?"
